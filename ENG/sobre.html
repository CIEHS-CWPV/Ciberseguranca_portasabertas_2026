Definition

Cybersecurity is the body of practices, technologies, and processes designed to protect systems, networks, and data from unauthorized access, cyberattacks, and damage. Its primary goal is to guarantee three fundamental pillars (The CIA Triad):

  • ● Confidentiality: Ensuring that only authorized individuals have access to information.
  • ● Integrity: Keeping data accurate and free from unauthorized modifications.
  • ● Availability: Guaranteeing that systems and services are accessible whenever needed.


Historical context

Concerns over digital security began to intensify in the 1980s and 1990s, when the first computer viruses started to spread. At that time, threats were relatively simple and often created as experiments or even pranks. With the commercialization and rise of the internet in the 2000s, attacks became more sophisticated, exploiting system vulnerabilities and tricking users through scams like phishing. Today, in the midst of the digital age, attacks such as ransomware and breaches into corporate networks can cause billions of dollars in damages and even compromise critical infrastructure. This evolution shows how cybersecurity has shifted from a purely technical concern to a strategic issue capable of impacting economies and even national stability.



Threats & Importance

Top Current Threats

  • ● Malware: Malicious software that infects systems.
  • ● Phishing: Attempts to trick users into stealing credentials.
  • ● Ransomware: Data hijacking with payment demands (extortion).
  • ● DDoS Attacks: Overwhelming servers to take down services.
  • ● Social Engineering: Psychological manipulation to gain unauthorized access.

Importance of Cybersecurity

  • ● Individual Protection: Preventing the theft of personal data, passwords, and banking information.
  • ● Corporate Security: Safeguarding corporate secrets and customer data.
  • ● National Defense: Protecting government systems, including Communications, Finance (banking), and the military.
  • ● Digital Trust: Allowing users to utilize online services with peace of mind.


Trends and The Future

The future of cybersecurity points to even greater challenges. Artificial intelligence(AI) is already being used to detect attacks more efficiently, but it is also being leveraged to create more sophisticated threats. Cloud computing, which distributes data across global servers, demands new protection strategies since data is no longer centralized in a single environment. The Internet of Things (IoT), with billions of connected devices, expands the attack surface, making every smart object a potential vulnerability point. In this scenario, the concept of cyber resilience emerges, moving beyond mere prevention: it is the ability to detect, respond to, and quickly recover from incidents, ensuring that systems continue to function even in the face of an attack.
